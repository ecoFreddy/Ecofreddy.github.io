Welcome to Fred Fu's page

email: fr.fu@ufl.edu

Fred Fu was born in Santa Cruz, California, and grew up in Guangzhou, China. During his childhood, he lived near the South China National Botanical Garden, a place he visited thousands of times. These early experiences fostered his appreciation for nature and inspired his long-standing interest in ecosystems and the environment.

He received his B.S. in Ecology from Zhejiang University under the guidance of Dr. Shuang Liang (梁爽) and Dr. Weile Chen (陈伟乐) and is currently a Research Master student at the University of Florida under the guidance of Dr. Jiangxiao Qiu.

Research Overview

Conceptual workflow of Fred's current research. Graph generated by AI.

My broader interests include ecological modeling, global change ecology, landscape ecology, remote sensing, and artificial intelligence.

My current research integrates process-based models, artificial intelligence, and Earth observations (e.g., remote sensing, flux tower and field measured data) to improve the understanding and prediction of carbon, water, and nitrogen dynamics in terrestrial ecosystems across scales. I aim to develop knowledge-guided machine learning frameworks that are accurate, interpretable, and transferable across space and time.

Selected Research Experience

1. Florida Grassland Hybrid Model Development

Role: Project Leader, supervised by Dr. Jiangxiao Qiu

I lead a project that develops a hybrid modeling framework for Florida grassland systems by integrating process-based modeling, Earth observation data, and machine learning. The project aims to improve the prediction of pasture productivity and environmental responses under different management conditions.

  • Collected and processed soil, weather, and management data, and reformatted these datasets as inputs for process-based model.
  • Developed a grazing module to better represent the effects of grazing management in APSIM-Agpasture, and adjusted bahiagrass species parameters to better capture the growth characteristics of Florida bahiagrass pastures.
  • Extracted and integrated Earth observations, including remote sensing data, eddy covariance observations, and field measurements, to calibrate and evaluate model performance at the local scale.
  • Aiming to integrate the calibrated process-based model into a knowledge-guided machine learning framework to improve large-scale prediction accuracy while increasing computational efficiency.
Work in Progress:Fu, F., Wu, R., Cha, J.-Y., Gedert, J., Boughton, E., Martens-Habbena, W., & Qiu, J. A Knowledge-Guided Machine Learning Framework for Modeling Productivity and Carbon Dynamics in Florida Subtropical Pastures. AGU Fall 2026. (Manuscript in prep)

2. Applying a Forest Landscape Model to Estimate Carbon Accumulation

Role: Undergraduate Project Leader, supervised by Liangyuan Cai (蔡良苑), Dr. Shuang Liang (梁爽), and Dr. Weile Chen (陈伟乐)

This project used the LANDIS-II forest landscape model to evaluate carbon accumulation patterns under different forest planting strategies. The study compared mixed plantations and monocultures to assess how species composition and landscape-level forest structure influence long-term carbon storage.

  • Collected species traits, life-history information, and ecological parameters to support species-level parameterization across different ecoregions.
  • Applied forest landscape modeling to simulate carbon accumulation under alternative plantation designs and species combinations.
  • Analyzed simulated carbon accumulation under mixed plantations and monocultures to evaluate how forest composition influences long-term carbon storage and to provide insights for climate change mitigation.

Work in Progress: Cai, L., Cui, W., Fu, F., Chen, W., Liang, S. Mixed-species plantations sustain long-term, climate-resilient carbon benefits over monocultures in subtropical China. (Manuscript in submission)

3. Field Experiments and Laboratory Analysis

Role: Project Assistant; Lab and Fieldwork Coordinator

Supported field experiments, laboratory analyses, and geospatial data processing for long-term agroecosystem and environmental monitoring.

  • Conducted field campaigns at the LTAR station, including soil sampling, root sampling, and monthly greenhouse gas sampling.
  • Supported laboratory analyses, including root washing, sorting, and weighing; soil sieving; dry matter measurement; and nutrient analysis.
  • Performed laboratory analyses: root washing, sorting, and weighing; soil sieving; dry matter determination; and nutrient analysis.
  • Acquired satellite imagery through Google Earth Engine and processed it in ArcGIS, including clipping to study sites and extraction of vegetation indices to complement field and laboratory measurements.
  • Coordinated monthly fieldwork with LTAR collaborators, confirming sites, schedules, and logistics, and organized team participation around members' availability.
    • Managed field equipment and organized collected samples with careful attention to accuracy, responsibility, and sample traceability.
